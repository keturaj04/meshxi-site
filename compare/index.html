COMPARE/OFFLINE MESSENGERS

MeshXi vs Briar, Bridgefy and bitchat

WRITTEN BY THE MESHXI DEVELOPER · LAST UPDATED 30 AUGUST 2026

Written by the people who make one of these, so read it with that in mind. The rule followed here: every row is something you can check for yourself, and where a rival is better it says so. Found a mistake? Tell us and it gets corrected.

The short answer

All four let phones talk with no internet. They differ in what they will carry over Bluetooth, what they ask of you to sign up, and how much of the code you are allowed to read.

Pick Briar

If auditable open source matters to you more than anything else. It is the most scrutinised app here and that is worth a great deal.

Pick MeshXi

If you want photos and voice notes to survive the offline hop, calls with no server on your own Wi-Fi, and Tor already switched on.

Pick bitchat

If you want the smallest, simplest thing that sends text over Bluetooth and does nothing else.

Where MeshXi is genuinely behind

  • The source is not public. Briar and bitchat can be read and audited by anyone. MeshXi cannot be, yet — and for a security app that is a real argument against it, not a footnote.
  • No iPhone version. Bridgefy and bitchat have one. The offline parts need real iOS work and there is no date to promise.
  • Fewer users means a thinner mesh. Every app here is only as good offline as the number of nearby phones running it.

What none of them can do

Any app promising otherwise is worth closing:

  • Bluetooth reaches tens of metres per hop. Range comes only from other phones in between relaying it. There is no long-range radio in your phone.
  • Make you anonymous in an absolute sense. These apps remove the account, the phone number and the server holding your history. They do not defeat someone holding your unlocked phone.
  • Work with nobody else nearby. An offline mesh with one user is one phone in a field.
